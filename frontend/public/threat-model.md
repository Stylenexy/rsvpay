# Threat model

Written from the point of view of someone trying to keep or steal money.

- **Unit** names a test in `contracts/src/tests.rs`. Run `cargo test` in `contracts/`.
- **Live** is a check in `docs/evidence/attacks-report.txt`, run by `scripts/attacks.mjs` against the deployed keyless contract. It counts only if the call failed **and** the state did not change.
- **Live P1 to P5** are the transfer and payout checks in `docs/evidence/transfer-checks-report.txt`, run by `scripts/transfer-checks.mjs` against the same contract on 11 November. They cover what a unit test cannot: a rolled-back lock, the resolver's real gas, a payout that really fails.

## Not defended

- **Amounts are public on chain.** An attached deposit is visible in any explorer. The app hides names and amounts from non-holders, but that is a screen, not secrecy.
- **A seller can empty free money before selling.** A buyer must read `hajatan(token_id)` first. Seeds are safe; free money is the seller's until the sale.
- **Guest keys live in the browser.** Clear the browser or change phones and the account is gone, unless the guest pressed **Save my key** and kept the file. **Restore a key** brings it back on any browser. The file is the whole key, so whoever holds it holds the account; a real product needs NEAR Auth instead.
- **Script on the page could read test keys.** The Content Security Policy narrows this. It does not remove it.
- **The testnet helper is a dependency.** If it is down or rate-limits, new guests cannot get an account. The limit is per IP (we hit "retry in 11 hours" after about a dozen accounts), so many guests on one Wi-Fi would run into it.
- **Transfers do not pay for the storage they add.** NEP-171 transfers attach one yoctoNEAR. A transfer to an account that holds no envelope yet adds about 256 bytes (0.0026 NEAR), and an envelope that went through `nft_transfer_call` keeps a 58-byte lock record after it settles. The contract pays both from its spare balance: 2.0 NEAR at block 276,848,214 on 9 November, beside 27.40 NEAR owed to 371 envelopes and 8.76 NEAR of storage ([spare-balance-report](https://github.com/Stylenexy/rsvpay/blob/master/docs/evidence/spare-balance-report.txt), `node scripts/spare.mjs`). Spending that spare on purpose costs more than it removes, because every extra holder needs an envelope its sender paid for. If it ran out, transfers to new holders and the last withdrawals would fail rather than take money from another envelope. A next contract reserves transfer storage when an envelope is opened and deletes settled locks (issue #23). Found by an outside review on 9 November.
- **Plain `nft_transfer` is not stopped by the lock.** While `nft_transfer_call` settles, the receiver is the owner on paper and can pass the envelope on with a plain transfer. The money stays locked until the resolver runs, but the resolver can no longer return the envelope, so the sender has lost it. This is how every NEP-171 token behaves: use `nft_transfer_call` only with a receiver you trust to settle. Unit `audit_plain_nft_transfer_is_not_gated_by_the_lock`.
- **The money goes wherever the token goes.** Sent to an account nobody controls, or to a contract that never calls `withdraw`, the free money and the kept shares sit there. Listing an envelope by transferring it into a marketplace contract gives that contract custody of the money.
- **A kept share is not proof of outside guests.** A holder can give into their own envelope from a second account and later reply it out to that account's envelope. That moves only their own money, but a buyer should read the entries, not treat `seed_locked` as a debt to strangers.
- **An owner's envelopes past the first 100 are not seen.** `hajatan_of` and the ring read an owner's first 100 envelopes. Nobody hosts that many, but anyone can send envelopes to a guest: with 100 sent ones in front, the ring misses that guest's real celebration or points at a sent one, which the guest holds, so a reply there still reaches them. `reciprocate` takes any envelope id and is not affected. A fix needs a new deployment.
- **Unused seeds stay locked.** If a guest never hosts, the seed never leaves. There is no expiry.
- **A host can ignore the ring.** "Later" changes nothing, and nothing forces a reply. The seed is a promise of the first step, not of the whole amount.
